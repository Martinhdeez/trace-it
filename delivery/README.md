# Delivery

What we hand the organisers, what produced it, and what we checked by hand.

The delivery itself lives in a **separate public repository**, as the challenge requires:
<https://github.com/mateo19182/la-caja-outcomes>. Its root holds exactly three files and
nothing else — no README, no licence, no code:

```text
la-caja-outcomes/
├── outcomes.jsonl          # batch 1, 500 lines
├── outcomes_lote2.jsonl    # batch 2, 40 lines (Saturday 18:00)
└── albertitos_plan.pdf     # Arquitectura + ADRs / trade-offs
```

The organisers clone it on **Sunday at 10:30 Madrid** and record the commit. They run a
private verifier over the two JSONL files: exactly one line per file of La Caja, and each
`result` among the expected ones. The check is binary and scores no points, but failing it
forfeits the prize.

**Which file is submitted as `outcomes.jsonl`: to be decided by the team.** The candidates:

- `outcomes_system.jsonl` (443 / 36 / 21): generated by the system, traceable;
- `outcomes_mateo_review.jsonl` (452 / 40 / 8): a hand review.

`outcomes_system.jsonl` (443 PAGAR / 36 NO_PAGAR / 21 ESCALAR) is generated by the system and
traceable: it is a byte-identical copy of `backend/reports/ocr-current/outcomes.jsonl`.
`outcomes_mateo_review.jsonl` (452 / 40 / 8) is a hand review, not produced by the system.
Whichever is chosen is copied to the delivery repository as `outcomes.jsonl`, the name the
challenge requires, and must stay identical to its copy here.

## What the hand review found

A local-only run and the shipped run disagreed on 19 files, all of them scans. Each was
read from the rendered document and checked against the supplier master, the order, the
ERP entry and the invoice's own arithmetic.

**Eighteen were correct as shipped.** The four rejections are real: the invoice names an
IBAN that differs from the supplier master, and two of them say so outright ("NUEVO NUM.
DE CUENTA", "Nuevo numero de cuenta desde este mes"). That is the supplier bank-change
trap, and the committee caught it.

| File | Shipped | Invoice IBAN | Master IBAN |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reimpresion_0712.pdf` | NO_PAGAR | ES47 2100 7788 1030 0044 5566 | ES27 0239 0806 6671 2233 4455 |
| `scan_016.pdf` | NO_PAGAR | ES71 3058 0022 7710 2233 8846 | ES93 6888 4400 1235 8890 0142 |
| `scan_018.pdf` | NO_PAGAR | ES58 0075 1120 4400 8812 3345 | ES76 2100 0813 6101 2345 6789 |
| `scan_029.pdf` | NO_PAGAR | ES65 0182 9977 4410 0033 2211 | ES93 6888 4400 1235 8890 0142 |

**One was wrong: `scan_011.pdf`, corrected from `NO_PAGAR` to `PAGAR`.** Read from the
document, NIF `B90233808` is Mensajería Rápida del Sur in the master and the invoice's
IBAN `ES93 6888 4400 1235 8890 0142` is the master's IBAN exactly. Order `PO-2026-0487` is
open for P011 at 1518.79, the invoice total; ERP entry `AS-00487` is PENDIENTE at the same
amount; VAT 263,59 and total 1.518,79 are both correct; the date 13/01/2026 is valid and
not future. Every clause of the norm passes, so the result is PAGAR. The committee had
rejected it on an IBAN mismatch that is not in the document.

The other fourteen are PAGAR and every check passes on each: IBAN equal to the master,
total equal to the order, ERP entry PENDIENTE, VAT and total arithmetically right.

## Why the wrong rejection was not caught

Worth knowing, because it bounds how much the file can be trusted.

The system already re-reads a field that disagrees with a source of truth
(`verification_triggers`, `backend/app/features/ingestion/payment_verification.py`): an IBAN
that does not match the master is exactly one of its triggers. But that function skips any
field already in `focused_verification`, because "already focused fields have consumed their
retry budget for this result". On a hard scan the IBAN is always focused first, so on the
documents that most need the second look, the second look never runs.

The obvious structural defence does not apply here: **all twelve master IBANs fail the
ISO 13616 mod-97 checksum**, as do the genuine mismatches. The accounts are synthetic, which
`app/common/normalization.py` already notes. A checksum validator is right in production and
worth nothing on this corpus.

What does separate the two cases is distance. A real bank change differs from the master in
**16 to 20 of 24 characters**; an OCR misread differs in one or two. A rejection that rests
on a near-identical IBAN is far more likely to be our misreading than the supplier's new
account, and belongs in the escalation queue rather than in a refusal.
