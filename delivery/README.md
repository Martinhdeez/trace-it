# Delivery

What we hand the organisers, what produced it, and what we checked by hand.

The delivery itself lives in a **separate public repository**, as the challenge requires:
<https://github.com/mateo19182/la-caja-outcomes>. Its root holds exactly three files and
nothing else — no README, no licence, no code:

```text
la-caja-outcomes/
├── outcomes.jsonl          # batch 1, 500 lines
├── outcomes_lote2.jsonl    # batch 2, 40 lines (Saturday 18:00)
└── albertitos_plan.pdf     # Arquitectura + ADRs / trade-offs
```

The organisers clone it on **Sunday at 10:30 Madrid** and record the commit. They run a
private verifier over the two JSONL files: exactly one line per file of La Caja, and each
`result` among the expected ones. The check is binary and scores no points, but failing it
forfeits the prize.

**Which file is submitted as `outcomes.jsonl`: to be decided by the team.** The candidates:

- `outcomes_system.jsonl` (443 / 36 / 21): generated by the system, traceable;
- `outcomes_mateo_review.jsonl` (452 / 41 / 7): a hand review against the updated ERP.

`outcomes_system.jsonl` (443 PAGAR / 36 NO_PAGAR / 21 ESCALAR) is generated by the system and
traceable: it is a byte-identical copy of `backend/reports/ocr-current/outcomes.jsonl`.
`outcomes_mateo_review.jsonl` (452 / 41 / 7) is a hand review, not produced by the system.
Whichever is chosen is copied to the delivery repository as `outcomes.jsonl`, the name the
challenge requires, and must stay identical to its copy here.

To read any decision with its evidence — the original PDF, the reason, every rule with its
verdict and the source rows it compared against — build the audit page
(`tools/audit_page/`).

## What the hand review found

A second manual pass rendered all 29 scans again and checked their printed fields against
the supplier master, order, ERP entry and invoice arithmetic. It corrected three scan labels
in the earlier hand-reviewed candidate.

- `scan_004.pdf`: `ESCALAR` to `PAGAR`. Every required field is legible and every check passes.
- `scan_017.pdf`: `ESCALAR` to `PAGAR`. Every required field is legible and every check passes.
- `scan_011.pdf`: `PAGAR` to `ESCALAR`. At 600 dpi the invoice prints `ES83...`, while the
  master prints `ES93...`. The one-character difference invokes the near-miss review rule.

The ERP update also changes `factura_4635.pdf` from `PAGAR` to `NO_PAGAR`: order
`PO-2026-0071` is now `PAGADA`.

The four scan rejections are real. Each invoice names an IBAN that differs substantially
from the supplier master, and two say that the bank account changed.

| File | Shipped | Invoice IBAN | Master IBAN |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reimpresion_0712.pdf` | NO_PAGAR | ES47 2100 7788 1030 0044 5566 | ES27 0239 0806 6671 2233 4455 |
| `scan_016.pdf` | NO_PAGAR | ES71 3058 0022 7710 2233 8846 | ES93 6888 4400 1235 8890 0142 |
| `scan_018.pdf` | NO_PAGAR | ES58 0075 1120 4400 8812 3345 | ES76 2100 0813 6101 2345 6789 |
| `scan_029.pdf` | NO_PAGAR | ES65 0182 9977 4410 0033 2211 | ES93 6888 4400 1235 8890 0142 |

Twenty scans are `PAGAR`; all of their visible fields pass. Five scans remain `ESCALAR`:
`copia_2026_0518.pdf`, `fax_2026_0411.pdf`, `scan_021.pdf` and `scan_023.pdf` have identifiers
that the damage prevents us from reading reliably, while `scan_011.pdf` has the one-character
IBAN near miss. The two native duplicate-order invoices account for the other two escalations.

All twelve original master IBANs fail the ISO 13616 mod-97 checksum because the corpus uses
synthetic accounts. The checksum cannot settle `scan_011.pdf`; the printed mismatch and the
near-miss rule are the relevant evidence.
